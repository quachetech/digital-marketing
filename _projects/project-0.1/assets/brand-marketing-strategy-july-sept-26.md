## Part 2 — Who You Serve

### The One Person
A complete beginner standing at the threshold of digital marketing. They have heard about it — maybe through a course, maybe through social media, maybe through a friend. They want to start. But they are stuck.

Not because they lack intelligence. Because they lack a realistic picture of what starting actually looks like.

They have likely only seen two things: polished success stories that make it look easy, and overwhelming technical content that makes it look impossible. Neither prepares them for the real thing.

### What They Are Feeling
- Fear of looking foolish
- Confusion about where to actually begin
- Self-doubt about whether they are capable
- Skepticism about whether it actually works
- Unrealistic expectations — either too high or too low

### What They Are Searching For
- Someone who remembers what Day 1 felt like
- Permission to be imperfect and still move forward
- A realistic sequence — not a highlight reel
- Proof that it is possible from a position similar to theirs

### What They Are NOT
- Someone already in the digital marketing space
- Someone choosing between courses
- Someone looking for advanced strategy
- Someone who needs to be convinced digital marketing exists

## Part 3 — The Competitive Landscape

### The Coca-Cola Principle Applied
Your real competition is not other digital marketing educators. Your real competition is the **alternatives a beginner uses instead of following you** — the things they do that delay or prevent them from starting.

### Your Real Competitors

**Competitor One — The YouTube Rabbit Hole**
Free, endless, and completely overwhelming. A beginner searches "how to start digital marketing" and gets 10,000 results with no sequence, no hand-holding, no one saying "start here first." They consume for weeks and never start.
*The loophole:* Information without direction produces paralysis, not action.

**Competitor Two — The Paid Course**
Structured but expensive. Often taught by someone who forgot what Day 1 felt like. The beginner buys it, gets overwhelmed by the volume of content, loses momentum, and quits before they ever post anything.
*The loophole:* Knowledge without accompaniment doesn't produce action for most beginners.

**Competitor Three — Guru Content**
Polished, aspirational, highlight-reel heavy. It sells the destination without showing the road. The beginner feels inspired for 48 hours, then paralysed again because they still don't know what to actually do on Day 1.
*The loophole:* Inspiration without sequence doesn't stick.

**Competitor Four — Doing Nothing**
The biggest competitor of all. The beginner decides it's too complicated, too risky, or not the right time. They stay on the other side of the threshold indefinitely.
*The loophole:* Inaction feels safe but costs them everything they actually want.

### What All Four Have in Common
None of them give the beginner **sequence + honesty + recency + proof simultaneously.**

That gap is your entire brand.

## Part 5 — Brand Positioning

### The Core Belief
Most beginner digital marketing content either overwhelms with everything at once or oversimplifies into uselessness. The real barrier is not lack of information. It is fear dressed up as confusion. Someone who just crossed the threshold is a better guide than someone who forgot what the doorway felt like.

### The Brand Promise
Honest. Sequenced. Real-time. No fast-money promises. No guru performance. Just what actually happens in the first few weeks, from someone living it right now.

### The Brand Voice
- Talks like a friend, not a teacher
- Admits what it doesn't know
- Celebrates small wins without performing them
- Calls out the real barriers without catastrophising
- Uses analogies and plain language — never jargon for its own sake

### The Free vs Paid Line
Free content is not competing with paid products. It is the proof that makes paid products trustworthy.

**The test:** Am I giving them the map, or am I doing the walking for them?
- Map — terrain, what to expect, how to start = free, always
- Walking — the prompts, the community, the done-with-you resources = the paid layer

## Part 6 — Marketing Strategy

### The Platform
TikTok. Primary and only channel for the first 90 days. Fully organic — no paid ads, no paid tools.

**Why TikTok:**
- 1.12 billion daily active users
- Interest graph — zero followers can reach millions if content matches viewer interest
- The most level playing field currently available for organic beginner growth
- Generation 3.0 search engine — your audience is already searching digital-marketing-adjacent content

### How Attention Becomes Action — The GPS Framework
Content must be a **Necessity** (they need this), **Transformative** (it changes something for them), and a **Profitable** presence in the niche (valuable enough that they return).

Every piece of content should move someone through one of three demand elements:

| Element | What It Does | Content Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Desire** | Shows what's possible | Transformation, journey, before/after |
| **Belief** | Eliminates doubt | Lessons, lived experience, myth-busting |
| **Action** | Clear next step | CTA content, offers, invitations |

All three must rotate. Desire-only builds reach without conversion. Belief-only builds trust without movement. Action-only feels pushy and unearned.

### The Funnel

```
Stranger on FYP
  → stops scrolling (hook works)
  → watches to the end (content holds attention)
  → follows (wants more)
  → consumes multiple posts (trust builds)
  → downloads IGNITION (first conversion — email captured)
  → stays for ongoing content (community forms)
  → buys first paid product when ready (Day 76–90)
```

### The 90-Day Brand Goals — In Order
1. **Awareness** — people discover the account exists
2. **Visibility** — people see it consistently enough to remember it
3. **Community** — people feel connected enough to engage and return
4. **Sales** — people trust enough to pay

This order is the roadmap. Each phase is the precondition for the next. Do not skip phases.

### CTAs by Phase — The KTL Alignment
Content is never created for its own sake. Every piece serves the bigger strategy, and every phase needs its own CTA — even the phases with no product to sell. People buy because of Know, Trust, Like (KTL). Each brand goal maps directly onto one stage of that sequence, and the CTA in each phase must ask for exactly the behaviour that stage is built to earn — never more, never less.

| Phase | KTL Stage | What the CTA Must Earn | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness (1–30) | Know | Follows, Saves | Recognition — become retrievable, the account they can find again |
| Visibility (31–60) | Know to Trust | Comments, Shares, Guide downloads | Comments prove engagement; the guide download is the first real trust transaction |
| Community (61–75) | Trust to Like | DMs, Discord joins | Moving from public engagement to a closer, chosen space |
| Sales (76–90) | Like to Action | Purchases | Trust and Like are spent here — this is where the relationship converts |

**The commitment escalation principle:** each phase's CTA asks for slightly more than the last — never two rungs at once, never less than the phase has earned. Save → Follow → Comment → Share → DM/Join → Purchase.

**Phase 1 — Awareness CTAs (Know):**
- Save-oriented (Lessons): *"Save this so you don't lose it when you need it"*
- Follow-oriented (Journey/Mindset): *"Follow if you want to watch this actually happen"*
- No comment-bait, no product mentions, no links

**Phase 2 — Visibility CTAs (Know deepening into Trust):**
- Comment-oriented (Mindset): *"Comment if this is you"*
- Share-oriented (Journey): *"Send this to someone who needs to hear it today"*
- Guide CTA from Day 31 (Lessons): *"Comment IGNITION for the free guide"*

**Phase 3 — Community CTAs (Trust into Like):**
- *"DM me IGNITION to join the group"* or *"Comment IGNITION if you want in"*
- Stories-driven CTAs matter more here — the human content makes someone comfortable enough to want a closer space

**Phase 4 — Sales CTAs (Like into Action):**
- Direct offer: *"Link in bio — this is what I built for exactly where you're at"*
- Urgency/close-sequence CTAs per launch mechanics (pre-launch tease → open-cart → urgency → close)

## Part 7 — Phase Sub-Goals

Brand goals are directional but not checkable day to day. Sub-goals turn each phase into something measurable week to week — a scoreboard, not just a hope.

### Phase 1 — Awareness (Days 1–30)
1. Format lock held — carousel only, zero exceptions, all 30 days
2. Fingerprint building — post daily, no more than 1 missed day per week
3. Analytics fluency in progress — check TikTok Studio at least 3x/week, log one observation each time
4. Voice and identity emerging — by Day 30, able to name the natural content lean
5. Geography correcting — viewer location data shifting toward English-speaking regions by Week 2
6. Zero promotion held — no CTA beyond follow/save, no guide mentions, no links

### Phase 2 — Visibility (Days 31–60)
1. Format expansion working — video introduced without carousel performance dropping
2. CTA live and converting — guide downloads tracked weekly against pace targets
3. Comment engagement rising — track comments-per-post trend, not just views
4. Lives used with intention — if invited, at least one Live run with deliberate structure
5. Repeat viewers identified — track which commenters/followers return more than once

### Phase 3 — Community (Days 61–75)
1. Discord accountability space live — created and soft-invited to engaged followers
2. 15-day free cohort run — using IGNITION guide as shared curriculum
3. Canva assets finished — all three prompt products fully designed, ready to sell
4. List size on pace — 2,000–3,000 checkpoint tracked weekly
5. Avatar re-validated — revisit comment mining bank, ask whether the one person has shifted

### Phase 4 — Sales (Days 76–90)
1. Launch sequence built before Day 76 — not written on the fly
2. First sale within 72 hours of launch
3. 95-sale pace tracked weekly, not just checked at Day 90
4. Subscription bridge live — cohort graduates offered $30/month Discord as natural continuation
5. Post-launch retro scheduled — fixed date to compare actual results to this plan

### Three Pillars

| **Journey** | Real-time build-in-public documentation | Belief |
| **Lessons** | Educational — frameworks, how-to, definitions | Belief + Action |
| **Mindset** | Fear, doubt, self-talk, emotional terrain | Desire + Belief |

### Five Sub-Topics
1. **Getting Started** — first steps, tool stack, realistic expectations
2. **Algorithm & Platform Reality** — how TikTok works, what's normal vs a real problem
3. **Mindset & Fear** — self-doubt, second-guessing, staying in motion
4. **Content & Consistency** — actually creating and posting
5. **First Sale / Survival** — the early weeks, debunking fast-money promises

### The Direct Resonance Strategy
Your audience is already searching digital-marketing-adjacent content on TikTok. You do not need to lure them from somewhere else with unrelated content. Speak directly to the search intent.

**This overrides any advice to create unrelated content in the first 30 posts.** That advice is built for audience migration — for creators whose real topic doesn't naturally attract their buyer. Your buyer is already in the pond. Speak to them directly.

### The Search-First Posting Approach (Days 1–30)
Do not announce yourself. Do not introduce yourself. Slip into the space quietly and solve a real problem immediately. The origin story and meet-and-greet wait until Day 30 when there is an audience that has a reason to care.

The goal in Phase 1 is to rank on searches — to be one of the first results a person sees when they search the niche or the topic in your posts. Every post is SEO-optimised regardless of pillar or sub-topic. Value first. Identity later.

## Part 9 — Tactics and Implementation

### Platform Mechanics — Account Three

**Pre-Posting Protocol (Days 1–7):**
- Zero posts
- Daily engagement with English-language digital marketing for beginners content
- Watch videos fully, leave genuine specific comments, follow niche creators
- Search niche keywords daily to reinforce the account's interest signal
- Cold-Start Override executed at account creation

**Format Rule (Days 1–30):**
Carousel only. One format locks the seed audience into one neural pipeline. Carousels outperformed video 8x in Account One data.

**Format Rule (Days 31–60):**
Carousel and video alternating. Introduce Lives if account receives an invitation.

**Posting Cadence:**
Once daily at midnight local time. Midnight bypasses the local Portuguese-language seed pool.

**The No-Promotion Rule (Days 1–30):**
Zero commercial content. Zero IGNITION mentions. Zero Beacons links. Zero CTAs beyond save/follow.

**Content Production Rules:**
- Every piece freshly recorded — no reused files from Account One or Two
- Scripts and captions safe to reuse or adapt
- Any post referencing specific stats must be updated to Account Three's real numbers before recording

### The Three-Tier SEO System
Every post needs keywords at three levels:
- **Spoken** — say keywords out loud in the video
- **On-screen** — keywords in text overlays
- **Caption** — rich, keyword-dense, written as valuable content in its own right

### Hashtag Pyramid
- Hashtag 1: Broad niche (#digitalmarketing)
- Hashtag 2: Specific niche (#digitalmarketingforbeginners)
- Hashtag 3: Post topic (changes per post)

### Comment Mining System
Go to large accounts in the niche. Read the comments. Screenshot real pain points and questions. These become content briefs written by the target audience.

**Priority content opportunities:**
- "How do I begin selling digital products?" (232 likes — highest signal)
- "I just bought my first digital product, how do I sell it?"
- "I run out of content ideas / content is hard"
- "Every way I turn it's no results"
- "Send me a funnel that works for Africans"
- Saturation fear — "this feels oversaturated"
- "Sick of marketers who don't want to help"

### Stories Strategy
Stories are for **humanity and relatability** — not content repurposing. The page is the professional. The stories are the human.

### The Discord Accountability Community
An accountability space, not a coaching program. Members work through the IGNITION guide on their own. Once a month, one hour, a group call — wins, challenges, and possible solutions talked through together. Price: $30/month.

**The 15-Day Free Challenge (Days 61–75):** A single cohort, not rolling admission. Members move through IGNITION together, post daily wins and struggles in Discord, and are encouraged through it. This window doubles as Phase 3 and as the strongest pre-launch trust-building activity available.

**Bridge to paid:** Since the free challenge and the paid space are the same Discord, the transition is a continuation, not a cold pitch — *"want to keep this going? $30/month keeps the space and the monthly call open."*

**Revenue note:** At $30/month this compounds with retention — 10 members is $300/month recurring. Judge it on its own timeline, not against the Day 90 number.

## Part 14 — Curriculum Roadmap: What to Study and When

### The Core Principle
Study just ahead of what each phase demands — not everything at once. This matches your own Classroom Before Cashflow principle and avoids the Two Masters trap.

### Foundation Skill — Study Now
**Analytics Literacy** — reading TikTok Studio data correctly. This is the skill that tells you whether every other phase is actually working. Start lightly now so you are fluent by Day 45.
*How:* TikTok Studio is the classroom. Open it daily. Ask one question per session. Log your interpretation in the experiment log.

### Phase One (Days 1–30) — Study This First
1. **Copywriting Fundamentals** — hooks, AIDA, PAS, scroll-stopping in 3 seconds. *How:* Search "AIDA copywriting explained" on TikTok/YouTube. Reverse-engineer your best captions.
2. **Basic SEO and Keyword Thinking** — why search intent matters beyond the keywords already mined. *How:* Creator Search Insights, 15 minutes per week.
3. **Platform Algorithm Literacy** — shared logic across platforms. *How:* Your tiktok-algorithm-research-v2.md is the foundation. One new source per week.
4. **Personal Branding and Positioning Fundamentals** — formal theory to name what you've already built. *How:* StoryBrand by Donald Miller, Al Ries' Positioning — summaries only. Read to recognise, not replace.
5. **Content Batching and Production Workflow** — scripting in batches, file naming, MD5-safe organisation. *How:* Your current 15-video banking session is the curriculum. Document the workflow as you build it.

### Phase Two (Days 31–60) — Study Before Day 31
1. **Email Marketing Fundamentals** — sequences, list hygiene, open/click metrics. *How:* Beacons and Mailchimp free guides. Reverse-engineer your existing automation.
2. **Video Script Pacing** — copywriting for spoken video differs from carousel text. *How:* Read one carousel script aloud. Notice where it feels unnatural.
3. **Content Repurposing Systems** — one idea into five formats. *How:* Map three existing pieces into every format they could become.

### Phase Three (Days 61–75) — Study Before You Arrive Here
1. **Community Management Basics** — what makes a small group engage vs go silent. *How:* Observe 2–3 active communities in adjacent niches. Document what the host does.
2. **Audience Research Deepening** — systematically updating the avatar as real data arrives. *How:* Review comment mining bank monthly. Ask: has my one person shifted?

### Phase Four (Days 76–90) — Study Before You Arrive Here
1. **Launch Mechanics and Launch Copywriting** — pre-launch, open-cart, urgency, close. *How:* Search "product launch sequence email." Map against your Beacons automation.
2. **Sales Psychology** — objection handling, the mechanics of KTL converting to purchase. *How:* Cialdini's influence principles — summaries. Apply each one to your own funnel.
3. **Pricing and Offer Structure** — value-stacking and anchor pricing. *How:* Study three competitors' pricing pages. Apply the lens to your own $27/$47/$67 structure.

### The Sequencing Principle
Study the skill 1–2 weeks before the phase that needs it. This is your own Three-Phase Beginner Model applied to curriculum design. The canvas must speak before it is framed. The same is true of curriculum.
