## Details & Deep Dives

### The GPS Framework — Be a GPS for Your Audience

The central metaphor of the session: as a digital product creator, your job is to be a GPS for your audience. A GPS has three qualities that every sellable digital product should mirror:

| GPS Quality | What It Means for Your Product |
|---|---|
| **Necessity** | It solves a real, pressing need — not a nice-to-have, but a must-have for the right person |
| **Transformative** | It moves the user from Point A to Point B — there is a clear before and after |
| **Profitable** | When built on necessity and transformation, the product becomes a genuine money-making asset |

> A product built to be a GPS does not need to be sold hard. The right person recognises it as something they need.

---

### The Five Elements of a Sellable Digital Product

These five elements apply regardless of experience level — beginner or advanced. A digital product that includes all five is positioned to sell.

**1. In Demand**
The product must be something people are actively looking for. Not something you think they should want — something they are already searching for, asking about, or expressing a need for. Demand validation comes before product creation.

**2. Solves a Pressing Problem**
Not a vague problem. A *pressing* one — something the audience feels urgently, that creates real discomfort or frustration, and that they are motivated to resolve. The more pressing the problem, the more motivated the buyer.

**3. Transformative**
There must be a clear before and after. The product takes the customer from where they are to where they want to be. The transformation must be real, specific, and believable.

**4. Emotionally Connected**
The transformation is not just functional — it is felt. After using the product, what does the customer *feel*? Confident? Relieved? Empowered? Clear? The emotional outcome is what people remember and what drives word of mouth. Build the emotional connection into the product design and the marketing language.

**5. Specific**
One clear person. One clear problem. One clear solution.

Do not try to speak to everyone. Do not try to solve every problem. Narrow scope is not a limitation — it is the precision that makes the product feel like it was made for exactly the person reading about it.

> One problem + one solution + one person = a product guaranteed to succeed.

### The SRT Principle — People Buy Solutions, Results, and Transformations

People do not buy products. They buy what the product *does for them* — the solution it provides, the result it produces, and the transformation it creates.

This is true regardless of the product category:

| Product | What People Are Actually Buying |
|---|---|
| A dress | Confidence, how they'll feel wearing it, how they'll be perceived, the compliments, who they'll attract |
| A fitness programme | The body, the energy, the self-image on the other side |
| A beginner's guide | Clarity, direction, the version of themselves who is no longer confused |
| A course | The skill, the income, the freedom, the identity shift |

The product is the vehicle. The SRT is the destination. And the destination is what people pay for.

**Why this matters for marketing:**
When you have done the research to understand your customer's SRT before building the product, marketing becomes a translation exercise — you already know exactly what they are hoping for, fearing, and imagining. Your content simply communicates that back to them.

> Everybody is asking *what's in it for me?* Your content strategy, your marketing copy, and your product positioning should all be answering that question — directly, specifically, and repeatedly.

---

### Applying This to Content Strategy

The SRT principle does not only apply to product marketing. It applies to content creation itself.

Every piece of content should be built around:
- **One clear person** — who exactly are you speaking to in this video, post, or caption?
- **One clear problem** — what specific frustration, confusion, or desire does this content address?
- **One clear solution** — what does the audience walk away with after consuming it?

This is the same framework as product creation — applied to a single piece of content.

For the current stage of this journey: the one person is the beginner. The one problem is confusion about where and how to start a digital marketing journey. The one solution is the honest, experience-based perspective of someone who just started and is documenting what actually happens.

## Personal Deductions

**On profitability:**
Profit is achieving the goal. Money is one form of profit — not the only form. At this stage, growing an audience, building a community, and growing an email list are all forms of profit. A product can be designed to achieve multiple goals simultaneously — audience growth, community building, email capture, and revenue. All of those are profitable outcomes.

The question to ask of any product or piece of content: *is this making me profitable toward the goals I have set for this stage?*

**On the confidence to sell:**
The Five Elements and the SRT Principle together produce something unexpected — clarity about whether a product is actually good. When a product is in demand, solves a pressing problem, is transformative, emotionally connected, and specific — the question is no longer *will this sell?* The question is *who needs to find it?* That shift from doubt to directional confidence is what separates someone who hesitates at the point of selling from someone who simply puts their product where the right person can find it.

**On watching the live itself:**
Being in the space of an experienced creator conducting a live — even without eligibility to host one yet — is preparation. The format, the pacing, the way value was delivered alongside the promotional offer, the energy of real-time audience engagement — all of it goes in as data. When the time comes to host, it will not be entirely unfamiliar territory.
